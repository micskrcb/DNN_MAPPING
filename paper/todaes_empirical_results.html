Complete Empirical Benchmark Evaluation Report:
All Six TODAES Workloads Executed Under Matched Physical Simulator Regimes

Independent Hardware-Algorithm Research Team
Repository: micskrcb/DNN_MAPPING • Platform: Bitwise Evaluator on RTX GPU / Multi-Core CPU • November 2026
Abstract—This report documents the exhaustive empirical evaluation across all six neural network placement workloads defined in Wu et al. (ACM TODAES 2020): AlexNet (CONV and FC), VGG16 (CONV and FC), and ResNet50 (CONV and FC). Using our bitwise-exact physical performance simulator ($4\times 4$ multi-chip array, 4,096 cores, 400 MHz clock, 64 GB/s on-chip NoC, 100 GB/s off-chip XY gateway routing), we execute the complete suite across Sequential Baseline (BS), Random Search (RS), and Simulated Annealing (SA). We record the exact microsecond execution latencies, analyze the architectural causes of optimization headroom, and contrast empirical findings against published paper claims.

3. Deep Architectural Takeaways

1. VGG16-CONV Routing Sensitivity: On VGG16-CONV (1,024 tasks across 4 chips), Random Search scatters tasks randomly across the mesh, causing severe gateway bottlenecks ($2,244.357\,\mu$s). Simulated Annealing clusters connected convolution stages locally, recovering a latency of $786.964\,\mu$s (a 64.94% reduction vs. RS and 38.22% vs. sequential baseline).
2. FC Cross-Chip Optimization: On communication-dominated FC layers (AlexNet-FC and VGG16-FC), cross-chip link contention dictates performance. SA optimizes AlexNet-FC by 36.21% ($3.920 \to 2.501\,\mu$s) and VGG16-FC by 23.57% ($6.292 \to 4.809\,\mu$s). On extended horizons, Contextual Bandit SA (CB-SA) further improves these to $2.008\,\mu$s and $4.263\,\mu$s.
3. Theoretical Bounds and Zero-Headroom Benchmarks: ResNet50-CONV ($588.784\,\mu$s) and ResNet50-FC ($5.470\,\mu$s) demonstrate that placement algorithms cannot improve circuits whose bottleneck stage is strictly compute-bound or contention-free, validating theoretical pipeline constraints.
